PERIOD 1 First Bell

Bright minds. Healthy Suns. Fully supported.

Welcome back, TRACE. However this summer treated you, September arrives the same way every year: fast, loud, and full of other people's needs. The research on this is not subtle. The single strongest predictor of whether school staff make it to June with something left in the tank is not passion or resilience — it's whether boundaries got set in the first six weeks.

So this issue is short on inspiration and long on tools. Five of the things below are interactive, and they remember where you left off. Use them from your phone in the parking lot at 3:15, which is honestly the most likely place any of this will happen.

September also carries weight. It's Suicide Prevention Awareness Month and National Recovery Month, with World Suicide Prevention Day on the 10th. We've put real resources in the Support period below — not as a formality, but because the people who spend all day noticing how students are doing are frequently the last to be asked how they're doing.

PERIOD 3 Passing Period

Four minutes you already have

Between classes, between meetings, in the car before you drive home. Three tools that fit in the gaps.

The Two-Minute Bell · box breathing

Ready

Four counts in, hold, out, hold

Box breathing is what dispatchers and paramedics use to steady themselves mid-shift. Four counts in, four holding, four out, four holding. Three or four rounds is enough to drop your heart rate before you walk into a hard conversation.

PERIOD 6 Prep Period

Boundaries, burnout, and where to get real help

Six-week self-check

How are the boundaries holding?

Six statements, thirty seconds. This is a reflection prompt, not a diagnosis or a screening tool — and nothing you tap is sent anywhere.

If today is heavy

Call or text 988

The Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is free, confidential, and staffed 24/7 — for you or for someone you're worried about. You do not have to be in crisis to call. September is Suicide Prevention Awareness Month, and the point of saying this out loud is that the number should already be in your phone before you need it.

Included with your benefits

VEBA Resource Centers

Care coordination, health coaching, and mental and emotional health support — in person or virtual, at no cost to CalVEBA members. The newest center opened in Chula Vista this spring, alongside the existing locations. The Advocacy Team also handles appointment scheduling, billing questions, and quality-of-care issues so you don't have to.

vebaresourcecenter.com

What actually helps

  • Pick two evenings a week where work stays at school. Two. Not five.
  • Debrief a hard class with one colleague the same day, before it calcifies.
  • If you lead a team: stop sending 11 p.m. emails. Staff read your hours as permission.
  • Keep a two-line log of what went right. It's the cheapest counterweight to a rough week.

Passed along

Disagreeing without wrecking anything

Boundaries are half of it. The other half is what happens when two people who both care about the same kids want opposite things — in a team meeting, in an IEP, in the hallway after a hard class.

This one is worth reading twice: the point of a disagreement is understanding, not winning. Taking a break when emotions run high isn't avoidance, and repair after the fact isn't weakness. If a conversation this week is going to be a hard one, look at the list first.
