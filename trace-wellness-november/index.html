BLOCK 01 Front desk

Enough

November hands you a holiday about gratitude and then gives you the darkest drive home of the year to feel it in. Those two things do not go together, and pretending they do is exhausting.

Gratitude gets handed to school staff as one more assignment. Be thankful. Write three things. Model it for the kids. And there is real research behind it — but the version that works is not the performance. It is the noticing.

Noticing is small and specific. Not I am grateful for my job. More like: the copier worked on Tuesday. A kid said thanks without being prompted. Somebody left the good pens out. That is the size of thing this issue is after, because that is the size of thing that actually holds up a hard month.

So this letter is one quilt you fill in a square at a time, a check-in for the people going home to a second shift of care, and a dish you can carry into a potluck without apologizing for it. Skip whatever you do not need. Skipping is allowed here.

Everything on this page saves to your own device and nowhere else. No accounts, no tracking, nothing reported to anyone.

If you only do three things

  1. Finish open enrollment by Friday, November 6. It closes this week with no late window, and what you pick holds for all of 2027.
  2. Stitch your first quilt square. One line about something you noticed today. No streak, no penalty for a blank square.
  3. Put a hold on The God of the Woods. Our first book together. Free from the library, the audiobook counts, and there is no meeting.

Everything else is here when you need it: a check-in for caregivers, thirty miles before the pie, a potluck dish that travels, and the checkups worth booking.

And one thing worth knowing: daylight saving ended November 1. If this week feels heavier for no reason you can name, that is the reason. Get ten minutes of outdoor light before school if you can.

    Your November

      Why specific beats profound

      “I am grateful for my family” is true every single day, which is exactly why writing it down does nothing. Your attention does not move. “My kid put their own shoes on without being asked” moves it — you had to go back and find that, and going back and finding it is the whole exercise.

      BLOCK 03 The second shift

      For the ones who clock out and clock in

      November is National Family Caregivers Month. A real share of this staff leaves campus and goes straight to a parent, a partner, a child with high needs, or all three. If that is you, this block is only for you. Five statements — tap the ones that are true this month.

        One thing you could hand to somebody else

        Not the whole load. One errand, one call, one Saturday. Caregivers are famously bad at this, and writing it down is how it stops being hypothetical.

        Saved on this device.

        You are already paying for this

        Start with VEBA

        California Schools VEBA includes counseling, one-on-one health coaching, and an advocacy team that will take on a claim denial for you — including denials on a family member's care. Caregivers use almost none of it, usually because nobody told them it applied to this. Advocacy Team: 888-276-0250, weekdays 8–5, or CalVEBA.org/Contact.

        Local and free

        • Southern Caregiver Resource Center — San Diego County. Free care planning, counseling, and respite grants for family caregivers.
        • Aging & Independence Services — the county's front door for in-home support and adult protective services.
        • Family Caregiver Alliance — statewide, with condition-specific guides written in plain language.

        Also worth knowing: California Paid Family Leave can cover time off to care for a seriously ill family member, not just a new baby. Payroll can tell you how it interacts with your sick leave.

        If it is heavier than a hard month

        988

        Call or text, any hour, any day. Caregiver burnout is one of the most common reasons people reach that line, and reaching it early is the entire point of it existing. Free and confidential.

        BLOCK 04 Move

        Thirty miles before the pie

        One mile a day, and the last one can be a Thanksgiving morning 5K with a few thousand other people. The dark makes this harder than October did — so move the walk to lunch, or to the ten minutes between the last bell and the drive.

        0.0 miles of 30

        Just for you — kept on this device, not reported anywhere. Nobody is checking your number but you.

        Walking in the dark, sensibly

        • Take the light while it is there. A ten-minute lunch loop now beats a well-intentioned 6 p.m. walk that will not happen.
        • Something reflective. Drivers are as tired as you are and the sun is in their eyes at 4:45.
        • Lit and flat. Liberty Station, the Mission Bay path, and most high school tracks are open and lit after hours.
        • Morning is the cheat code. Ten minutes of outdoor light before 10 a.m. does more for the time-change slump than anything you can do in the evening.

          BLOCK 06 The reading room

          One book, one month, no meeting

          New this month: an open invitation to read one book together. The clocks went back, it is dark by the time most of us leave, and that is not entirely a bad thing. There is nowhere to be, nothing to prepare, and no night you have to give up. The point is that you have something to talk about with the person you are already standing next to.

          November's book

          The God of the Woods

          Liz Moore · 2024

          Summer 1975, a private camp in the Adirondacks. A counselor does a morning bunk check and a thirteen-year-old girl is gone. Fourteen years earlier, her older brother disappeared from the same woods.

          It was picked for one reason: it pulls you along. Nobody needs homework in November. This is the book you look up from and realize it is eleven o'clock.

          One honest heads up — a missing child sits at the center of the story. If that is not what you need in your head this month, sit this one out. Nobody is taking attendance.

          How it works

          • Get it free. The San Diego Public Library has about 120 copies in the system, plus ebook and audiobook on Libby. The three hold lines are separate, so if one is long, try another.
          • The audiobook counts. So does listening on the drive in. There is no wrong way to get through a book.
          • No chapters, no schedule. Read at whatever pace your month allows, and the days around Thanksgiving are the first real stretch of time most of us have had since August. Falling behind is not a thing here, because there is nothing to fall behind.
          • No meeting. Ever. The conversation happens in the copy room, at lunch, in the parking lot, in the thirty seconds before a meeting starts.

          Later this month a spot opens up right here to post what you thought, the same way the song board does. Read first. We will send the link when it is live.

          BLOCK 07 Benefits desk

          The number you have not checked

          November is American Diabetes Month, Lung Cancer Awareness Month, and Movember all at once — which is a lot of ribbons for one page. Underneath all three is the same unglamorous ask: go get the number checked.

          Last call · Friday, November 6

          Open enrollment closes this week

          There is no late window. Whatever you pick holds for all of 2027, so ten minutes now beats twelve months of the wrong plan. If you have not looked yet, this is the week. Last in-person help: Wednesday, November 4, 2–5 pm, on the Ed Center front lawn at 4100 Normal St. District open enrollment page

          More than one in three American adults has prediabetes, and the large majority of them do not know it. It has no symptoms worth noticing. The only way it turns up is a blood test you have to go and ask for — and at that stage it is one of the most reversible things in medicine.

          The same is true of blood pressure, and of the lung screening that people who smoked for years qualify for and almost never claim. None of this is a lecture about willpower. It is a fifteen-minute appointment that your plan already covers at no cost to you.

          Preventive care is covered on every plan, whatever you pick this week, so nothing below waits on your elections.

          Thursday, November 19

          The Great American Smokeout

          The premise is one day, not forever, because forever is why people never start. Quitting help is covered on your plan, and the California Smokers' Helpline is free coaching by phone or text for anyone in the state. If a colleague mentions they are trying, the useful thing to say is nothing about their lungs and something about their week.

          Worth booking this month

          • An A1C or fasting glucose, if it has been more than three years.
          • A blood pressure reading that is not the one the pharmacy machine gave you.
          • Your flu shot, if November got away from you. It is free on every plan and you work in a building with several hundred children.
          • The physical you have moved twice. Movember exists because of exactly this.

          Before Friday the 6th

          What actually needs doing

          If you change nothing, your medical, dental, and vision carry over into 2027 as they are. Anything you do change starts January 1.

          Using an FSA? Sign up again.

          Flexible spending is the one thing that does not roll over. It starts at zero every year, and unused 2026 money does not carry into 2027. Elect it in PeopleSoft Employee Self-Service under Benefits > Benefits Enrollment. Step-by-step instructions

          Switching plans? Compare first.

          Plan changes and first-time enrollment happen in the same PeopleSoft screen. The side-by-side chart puts every plan's costs on one page, and the VEBA guide covers the free extras. Plan comparison chart · VEBA benefits guide

          Adding or removing someone? Use paper.

          This window you can add or drop a dependent with no life event required, but not online. Return the change form, plus proof of eligibility when you are adding someone. Enrollment/change form · Documents they accept

          Check your beneficiary.

          The district pays for a life insurance policy through The Hartford worth your annual salary. Make sure it would go to the right person; you can review and change that on The Hartford's BenSelect site. Bulletin No. 64 overview

          Stuck? Employee Benefits: employeebenefits@sandi.net or (619) 725-8130.
