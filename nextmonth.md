# Build this: the "Ideas for next month" form

A second Google Form, separate from the song form. This one collects what
should go *in* the next issue. Keep them separate — the song form is a
thirty-second thing people fill in from their phone, and mixing a longer
"what should we cover" ask into it will cost you song submissions.

Unlike the playlist, these answers are **not** published on the site. They come
to you and the committee, and you decide what makes the issue. That changes what
you can safely ask for: you can invite longer, more personal answers here than
you ever could on a public page.

---

## Settings

New form at [forms.new](https://forms.new).

- **Title:** Rise & Shine — what should next month look like?
- **Description:**
  > This letter is only useful if it's the one you'd actually open. Tell us what
  > belongs in the next issue. Everything here goes to the Wellness Committee —
  > nothing gets published without asking you first.
- **Settings → Responses:** turn on **Collect email addresses** (restricted to
  the district) so you can follow up on a good idea. Leave **Limit to 1 response**
  OFF — people should be able to come back with a second thought.
- **Settings → Presentation:** confirmation message
  > Got it. Thank you — genuinely. Ideas from staff are where most of this letter
  > comes from.

## Questions

Eight questions. Only the first is required. The whole thing should be
answerable in under two minutes by someone who only wants to say one thing.

| # | Question | Type | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What's your idea? | Paragraph | **yes** | The whole form in one box. Someone with exactly one thing to say can answer this and stop. |
| 2 | What kind of thing is it? | Checkboxes | no | Options below. |
| 3 | Is there a month it belongs in? | Dropdown | no | Options below. |
| 4 | If it's a recipe, or a place, or a link — drop it here | Paragraph | no | Help text: "Paste anything. A link, a recipe, an address, a name." |
| 5 | Would you want to help make it happen? | Multiple choice | no | Options below. |
| 6 | Is there someone on staff we should be featuring? | Short answer | no | Help text: "Someone quietly doing a good thing. We'll ask them before we print anything." |
| 7 | What's missing from the letter so far? | Paragraph | no | Help text: "Be blunt. This is the most useful question on the form." |
| 8 | Your name | Short answer | no | Help text: "Optional. Answers stay with the committee either way." |

### Q2 options — "What kind of thing is it?"

Checkboxes, so people can tick more than one. These mirror the sections the
letter already has, which makes it obvious where an idea would land:

- A recipe
- A challenge or something to try
- Something to move (a walk, a class, a stretch)
- A mental health or support topic
- An event worth putting on the calendar
- A person worth featuring
- A discount, perk, or benefit people don't know about
- Something else entirely

### Q3 options — "Is there a month it belongs in?"

Dropdown. The list matches the issues still to come:

- No particular month
- October
- November
- December
- January
- February
- March
- April
- May

### Q5 options — "Would you want to help make it happen?"

Multiple choice:

- Just the idea, thanks
- I'd help if you need me
- I'd want to write or run this one
- I'd rather stay anonymous on this

## Why it's shaped this way

**Question 1 does the real work.** Every other question is optional, and most
people will answer one or two. Long forms with eight required fields get
abandoned; long forms with one required field get finished.

**Question 5 exists to find your next committee member.** The committee is
recruiting — this quietly surfaces who is willing without putting anyone on the
spot in a meeting.

**Question 7 is the one to actually read.** "What's missing" gets you the honest
answer that "any ideas?" never does. Expect it to be the most uncomfortable and
most useful column in the sheet.

**Don't publish these answers.** Unlike the playlist, there's no public page for
this. If an idea does become a section, ask that person first — being quoted in
a staff-wide letter without warning is a bad surprise even when it's flattering.
