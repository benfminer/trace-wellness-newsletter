HOUR 01 Front desk

Doing less, on purpose

December is short and it is loud at the same time — the shortest days of the year land underneath the busiest calendar of the year. Something has to give. This issue is permission to decide in advance what that is, instead of finding out the hard way on December 18th.

Most wellness advice for this month is about adding things — add a gratitude practice, add a self-care ritual, add one more thing to an already full plate, and call the addition rest. It rarely works, because the plate was the problem.

So this issue only asks you to subtract. Pick a few things you are not doing this year, on purpose, and say so out loud. Take ten minutes of actual daylight instead of a wellness webinar about the importance of daylight. Make a soup that takes fifteen minutes of hands-on time. Notice, without diagnosing yourself, whether the dark is doing something to your mood — and know where to go if it is.

Everything on this page saves to your own device and nowhere else. No accounts, no tracking, nothing reported to anyone.

The subtract list

Eight common December obligations that nobody actually requires of you. Tap the ones you are dropping this year. Watching the line strike through is most of the point.

You are not doing 0 things this December

    Saved on this device only. Nothing here is submitted, shared, or visible to the committee.

    Why this works

    A list of things you are not doing sounds like a joke until you try to write one. Most people can name what they are supposed to do in December without thinking. Almost nobody has ever written down what they are allowed to skip. Naming it in advance is what makes skipping it in the moment feel like a decision instead of a failure.

    If the list fills up fast

    That is information, not a problem to fix today. A December where every obligation feels optional to drop is usually a December that was overbuilt to begin with — worth a look at what gets planned for next year, once this one is over.

    You do not owe December a performance of joy. Just your own, whatever size it comes in.

    HOUR 03 Move

    Ten minutes of actual daylight

    Not a step count, not a mile goal — this month the ask is smaller than that. Ten minutes outside, in real daylight, at some point in your day. It is one of the few things with real evidence behind it for the winter slump, and it costs nothing.

    This week

      Just for you — kept on this device, not reported anywhere.

      Where the ten minutes fits

      • Morning beats evening. Light before 10 a.m. does more for your circadian rhythm than the same light at 4 p.m. — a lot even, walk to your car the long way.
      • It does not have to be a walk. Eating lunch outside, on a bench, with your face actually toward the sky, counts.
      • Cloudy still counts. Outdoor light on an overcast December day is still many times brighter than the light inside your classroom.
      • Duty coverage works. If you already have recess or arrival duty outside, that is not extra — it is today's ten minutes, already spent.

      One-pot lentil soup with lemon

      Fifteen minutes of actual work, thirty on the stove while you grade or wrangle carpool. One pot, no side dishes required, and it freezes in portions for the weeks when cooking is one more thing you are allowed to subtract.

        Method

        Make ahead: keeps five days refrigerated and freezes well in portions — the lemon is brightest added fresh, so if freezing, hold it back and stir it in after reheating. Red lentils cook fastest and break down into the broth; green or brown lentils hold their shape and add 10–15 minutes.

        HOUR 05 Counselor's office

        The dark does something. Here is what to do about it.

        December is Seasonal Affective Disorder Awareness Month. This is not a screening and it will not diagnose anything — it is a short, private way to notice a pattern, the same rules as every reflection tool in this letter. If something here feels heavier than a seasonal dip, the resources below are real and covered.

          Covered, not extra

          VEBA counseling

          California Schools VEBA includes counseling sessions at no cost to you, separate from and in addition to anything through your health plan. Seasonal low mood is one of the most common, least dramatic reasons people use it — you do not need a crisis to justify the appointment. The support line reaches a counselor any hour of any day: (888) 625-4809.

          Worth trying this month

          • A 10,000-lux light box, used for 20–30 minutes in the morning — the best-studied non-medication tool for seasonal low mood.
          • Keeping the same wake time on weekends. The single biggest lever for winter mood is a stable circadian rhythm, not more sleep.
          • A vitamin D check at your next preventive visit — low levels are common in the winter and easy to correct.

          If it is heavier than a seasonal dip

          988

          Call or text, any hour, any day. Free and confidential. If the dark has taken more from you than energy and mood, this line exists for exactly that.

          HOUR 06 Benefits desk

          The unglamorous case for washing your hands

          National Handwashing Awareness Week runs the first full week of December, which sounds like the least interesting item in this whole letter. It is also, per capita, one of the highest-leverage things anyone in a school building can do all winter.

          You work in a building with several hundred people who touch their faces, share supplies, and are, on average, terrible at covering a cough. December and January are the peak of flu and cold season in San Diego, and staff absences track student absences almost exactly — when one goes up, so does the other, usually with a lag of about a week.

          None of this is about willpower or a poster over the sink. It is twenty seconds, soap, and doing it more often than feels necessary — after recess duty, after the copier, before you eat at your desk. The flu shot you may have put off in the fall is also still worth getting; it is free on your plan and effective well into the season it protects against.

          Covered at no cost to you

          • The flu shot, at any pharmacy or your doctor's office — no copay on any plan.
          • A sick visit for anything that looks like it might be more than a cold. Preventive care is not the only thing covered at no cost; a lot of urgent care is too, depending on your plan.
          • The VEBA advocacy team, if a claim from any of the above gets denied or mishandled — that is what they are for.

          Sick leave exists so that a cold does not become a building-wide outbreak by the second week of December. Using it when you are actually sick is not letting anyone down.
